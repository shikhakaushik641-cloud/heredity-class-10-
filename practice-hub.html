📘 NCERT — In-text & Exercises

The NCERT questions on heredity (in-text questions and the end-of-chapter exercise). Try them in your notebook first. 12 questions.

Q1NCERT in-text Q1Accumulation of variation📖 Revise · Tutorial 1
In an asexually reproducing species, trait A is in 10% of the population and trait B in 60%. Which trait is likely to have arisen earlier?
Why (b)? In asexual reproduction a trait spreads only by division, so the commoner trait has had more generations to spread.
Q2NCERT in-text Q2Importance of variation📖 Revise · Tutorial 1
How does the creation of variations in a species promote survival?
Model answer
If the environment changes (e.g. temperature rises or a disease spreads), individuals with suitable variations may survive while others die. So a varied population is less likely to be wiped out completely; the useful variation spreads in later generations.
How did you do?
Q3NCERT in-text Q3Dominant and recessive📖 Revise · Tutorial 2
How do Mendel’s experiments show that traits may be dominant or recessive?
Model answer
TT (tall) × tt (dwarf) gave only tall F₁ plants, although they carried the dwarf factor (Tt). In F₂, ¼ were dwarf again. So the tall trait expressed itself over the dwarf one (dominant), while dwarfness stayed hidden in F₁ (recessive) and reappeared in F₂.
How did you do?
Q4NCERT in-text Q4Independent inheritance📖 Revise · Tutorial 3
How do Mendel’s experiments show that traits are inherited independently?
Model answer
RRYY × rryy gave RrYy (round yellow). Selfing F₁ gave four types in F₂ in the ratio 9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green. Round-green and wrinkled-yellow are new combinations, which could appear only if shape and colour were passed on independently.
How did you do?
Q5NCERT in-text Q5Sex determination📖 Revise · Tutorial 4

How is the sex of the child determined in human beings?
Model answer
A mother has XX and gives an X in every egg. A father has XY: half his sperm carry X, half carry Y. X sperm + X egg → XX girl; Y sperm + X egg → XY boy. So the father’s chromosome decides, with a 50 : 50 chance.

Q6NCERT in-text Q7Acquired traits📖 Revise · Tutorial 1
Traits acquired during a lifetime are inherited because they change the DNA of germ cells.
False. Correct statement: Acquired changes occur in body (somatic) cells, not in germ-cell DNA, so they are not inherited.

Q7NCERT in-text Q8Small populations📖 Revise · Tutorial 1
Why are the small numbers of surviving tigers a cause of worry from the point of view of genetics?
Model answer
A small population has little genetic variation. If a disease or environmental change occurs, there may be no individuals with variations that let them survive, so the whole population could be wiped out.
How did you do?
Q8NCERT Exercise Q1Working out a genotype📖 Revise · Tutorial 2
Tall pea plants with violet flowers are crossed with short plants with white flowers. All progeny have violet flowers, but almost half are short. The tall parent is:
Why (c)? Half short → tall parent is Tt; all violet → WW.
Q9NCERT Exercise Q4Interpreting data📖 Revise · Tutorial 2
Children with light-coloured eyes are likely to have parents with light-coloured eyes. Can we say whether light eye colour is dominant or recessive?
Model answer
No. This shows only that the trait is inherited. To decide dominance we need crosses where the parents differ (one light, one dark) and the ratio of offspring in later generations.
How did you do?
Q10NCERT Exercise Q7Designing a cross📖 Revise · Tutorial 2

Outline a project which aims to find the dominant coat colour in dogs.
Model answer
Cross a pure black (BB) dog with a pure white (bb) dog. The colour shown by all F₁ pups is dominant (say black, Bb). Cross F₁ pups with each other: F₂ should show about 3 black : 1 white, confirming black is dominant.
How did you do?
Q11NCERT Exercise Q10Equal contribution📖 Revise · Tutorial 4
How is the equal genetic contribution of male and female parents ensured in the progeny?
Model answer
Gametes are made by meiosis, so each has one set — 23 chromosomes (22 autosomes + X or Y). Sperm (22 + X or 22 + Y) and egg (22 + X) fuse to form a zygote with 23 pairs, half from each parent.
How did you do?
Q12NCERT Exemplar styleMonohybrid F₂📖 Revise · Tutorial 2
A pure tall plant is crossed with a pure dwarf plant and F₁ is selfed. Which plants were missing in F₁ but reappeared in F₂, and in what ratio?
Why (b)? Dwarf plants vanish in F₁ and return as ¼ of F₂.

There is an inbuilt tendency to variation due to errors in DNA copying.

Variations in body (somatic) cells are passed on to the next generation.

Q1UDAAN VSA 1Acquired traits📖 Revise · Tutorial 1
“We cannot pass on to our progeny the experiences and qualifications earned during our lifetime.” Justify with reason and examples.
Model answer
Experiences and skills are stored in brain and body cells, not in the DNA of germ cells. Only germ-cell DNA reaches the next generation. A doctor’s degree or a musician’s skill is therefore not inherited.
How did you do?
Q2UDAAN VSA 3Mono vs dihybrid📖 Revise · Tutorial 3
Differentiate between a monohybrid and a dihybrid cross.
Model answer
Monohybrid: one pair of contrasting traits (e.g. tall × dwarf); F₂ 3 : 1. Dihybrid: two pairs at once (e.g. round-yellow × wrinkled-green); F₂ 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.
How did you do?
Q3UDAAN VSA 4Writing genotypes📖 Revise · Tutorial 2
Write the genotypes for: (a) purple flowers (heterozygous) (b) round seeds (homozygous) (c) green pods (heterozygous) (d) dwarf plants (homozygous).
Model answer
(a) Pp (b) RR (c) Gg (d) tt
How did you do?
Q4UDAAN VSA 5Haploid organisms📖 Revise · Tutorial 2
A bacterium is haploid. Can we use the terms homozygous and heterozygous for it?
Model answer
No. These terms describe the two copies of a gene in a diploid organism. A haploid bacterium has only one copy of each gene, so it can be neither homozygous nor heterozygous.
How did you do?
Q5UDAAN VSA 6Only daughters📖 Revise · Tutorial 4
A woman has only daughters. Analyse the situation genetically.
Model answer
Each daughter received an X from the father’s sperm (and an X from the mother). Every pregnancy has a 50 : 50 chance of X or Y sperm, so having only daughters is chance — and the sex is decided by the father’s chromosome, not the mother.
How did you do?
Q6UDAAN SA 3Test cross📖 Revise · Tutorial 2
An individual shows the dominant trait. How can you find out if it is homozygous or heterozygous? Use a cross to illustrate.
Model answer
Do a test cross with a homozygous recessive. TT × tt → all dominant. Tt × tt → 1 dominant : 1 recessive. Any recessive offspring proves the individual is heterozygous.
How did you do?

Q7UDAAN SA 7Rationale for independent assortment📖 Revise · Tutorial 3
Mendel’s dihybrid cross gave 4 types of individuals. How did he deduce independent assortment from this?
Model answer
If the two genes were always passed on together, F₁ gametes would only be RY and ry, and F₂ would show only the parental types (round yellow and wrinkled green). The appearance of new combinations (round green, wrinkled yellow) in a 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio showed that the genes sort independently.
How did you do?
Q8UDAAN LA 1Monohybrid in depth📖 Revise · Tutorial 2
(a) Why is F₁ always tall when a pure tall plant is crossed with a dwarf plant? (b) How is F₂ different from F₁, and why? (c) What conclusion can be drawn?
Model answer
(a) F₁ is Tt; T is dominant, so all are tall. (b) F₂ has tall and dwarf in 3 : 1, because ¼ of plants receive t from both parents (tt). (c) Alleles segregate during gamete formation without blending — the law of segregation.
