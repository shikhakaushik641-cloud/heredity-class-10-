How Traits Are Expressed and Sex Determination

From DNA to proteins to a tall plant — and why the father’s chromosome decides boy or girl.

🎯 By the end of this tutorial you will be able to

  • Explain how a gene controls a trait through a protein
  • Explain how equal genetic contribution of both parents is ensured
  • Show with a cross how the sex of a child is determined
  • Recognise incomplete dominance, codominance and other sex-determination systems

How Do Traits Get Expressed?

Cellular DNA is the information source for making proteins. A section of DNA that carries the information for one protein is the gene for that protein. Many proteins are enzymes that run the chemical reactions which build our features.

✍ NCERT example — plant height

Plants have a hormone that makes them grow tall. The amount of hormone depends on how well a particular enzyme works. If the gene gives an efficient enzyme, plenty of hormone is made and the plant is tall. If a change in the gene makes the enzyme less efficient, less hormone is made and the plant is short. So genes control traits through proteins.

Two copies in the body, one copy in each gamete

  • Each parent gives one copy of every gene, so the offspring has two copies for every character.
  • Germ cells are made by meiosis (reduction division), so each gamete carries only one set of genes, on half the number of chromosomes.
  • When two gametes fuse at fertilisation, the normal number is restored. This keeps the DNA of the species stable from generation to generation.
  • Genes sit on chromosomes. Each gamete gets one chromosome from each pair — maternal or paternal — at random, which shuffles traits and creates variation.
✍ NCERT Exercise 10

How is the equal genetic contribution of male and female parents ensured in the progeny?

Each gamete has 23 chromosomes — 22 autosomes plus one sex chromosome. The sperm brings 22 + X or 22 + Y; the egg brings 22 + X. The zygote gets 23 chromosomes from each parent, i.e. 23 pairs, with equal genetic material from both.

Sex Determination

Deciding whether a new individual becomes male or female is called sex determination. In some animals it depends on the environment: in some reptiles, the temperature at which the eggs are kept decides the sex, and snails can even change sex. In humans it is decided by genes.

  • Every child gets an X from the mother.
  • If the father’s sperm brings an X → XX → girl. If it brings a Y → XY → boy.
  • So the sex of a child is decided by the chromosome from the father, and the chance is 50 : 50 for every child.
⚠ A common myth

Blaming the mother for “only daughters” has no scientific basis. And each birth is a fresh 50% chance — a couple with six daughters still has a 50% chance of a girl next time.

Beyond the Textbook

These ideas are from the UDAAN Competitive Level. They are useful for Olympiads and for understanding, but are not in the CBSE Class 10 syllabus.

Incomplete dominance

In the snapdragon (Antirrhinum), red (RR) × white (rr) gives pink (Rr) F1 — neither allele is fully dominant. F2 gives 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white, so here the phenotypic and genotypic ratios are the same.

Codominance and multiple alleles — ABO blood groups

The ABO gene has three alleles: IA, IB and i. IA and IB are codominant (both show in IAIB = group AB); both are dominant over i. A gene with more than two alleles shows multiple alleles. Six genotypes give four blood groups.

Other sex-determination systems

🦗XX–XOGrasshopper: female XX, male has a single X (XO).
🐔ZW–ZZBirds: male ZZ, female ZW — the mother decides the sex.
🦋ZO–ZZSome moths and butterflies: female ZO, male ZZ.
🐝Haplo-diploidHoney bee: fertilised eggs (32 chromosomes) → females; unfertilised eggs (16) → males (drones).

Reveal the answer

(c) IBi — the O child (ii) must have received an i from the father.

Quick Check 4

Q1How many pairs of chromosomes are present in human beings?
23 pairs = 46 chromosomes: 22 pairs of autosomes + 1 pair of sex chromosomes.
Q2True or False: A child who inherits a Y chromosome from the father will be a girl.
False — XY is a boy.
Q3A gene controls a trait mainly by making a:
Genes code for proteins (often enzymes).
Q4The chromosomes that decide sex are called sex chromosomes or ?.
Allosomes; the others are autosomes.
