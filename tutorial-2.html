Mendel and the Monohybrid Cross

A monk, a garden of peas, and the 3 : 1 ratio that started genetics.

🎯 By the end of this tutorial you will be able to

  • Explain why the garden pea suited Mendel's experiments
  • Use genetic terms correctly
  • Work out F1 and F2 results of a monohybrid cross with a Punnett square
  • State the laws of dominance and segregation and use a test cross

Gregor Mendel — the Father of Genetics

Gregor Johann Mendel (1822–1884) was an Austrian monk. In his monastery garden he crossed garden peas, Pisum sativum, for about seven years (1856–1863). He published his results in 1866, but nobody understood their importance until 1900, when three scientists — Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns and Erich von Tschermak — independently rediscovered them.

💡 What made Mendel different

He was the first to use counting, statistics and mathematical logic in biology. He studied thousands of plants, so his ratios were reliable, and he checked his ideas over several generations.

Why the garden pea?

🔁Self-pollinatingFlowers are bisexual and self-fertilise, so pure lines (true-breeding plants) are easy to get.
✂️Easy to crossAnthers can be removed and pollen dusted by hand for controlled cross-pollination.
⏱️Short life cycleOne generation per growing season; easy to grow in large numbers.
🎭Clear contrasting traitsMany characters come in two sharply different forms — tall or dwarf, round or wrinkled.

HeterozygousTwo different alleles — makes two types of gameteTt

Alleles sit at the same place on a pair of homologous chromosomes — one chromosome of the pair comes from the mother and one from the father.

The Monohybrid Cross

A monohybrid cross follows one pair of contrasting traits. To cross two pea plants, Mendel removed the anthers of one flower before they matured (emasculation), covered it with a bag to keep out stray pollen (bagging), and then dusted it with pollen from the chosen father plant.

P: Tall (TT) × Dwarf (tt) → F1: all Tall (Tt)
F1 selfed: Tt × Tt → F2: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt → 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf

  • The phenotypic ratio in F2 is 3 : 1 (75% tall, 25% dwarf).
  • The genotypic ratio in F2 is 1 : 2 : 1 (TT : Tt : tt).
  • The dwarf trait disappeared in F1 but reappeared unchanged in F2 — there was no blending.
💡 The Punnett square

Devised by the British geneticist Reginald C. Punnett. Write one parent’s gametes along the top and the other parent’s down the side; each box is one possible offspring.

Mendel’s First Two Laws

◆ 1. Law of Dominance

Characters are controlled by units called factors (now called genes) that occur in pairs. When the two factors are different, one (the dominant) expresses itself and the other (the recessive) stays hidden.

◆ 2. Law of Segregation (purity of gametes)

The two alleles of a pair separate during gamete formation, so each gamete gets only one allele. The alleles do not blend — that is why the recessive trait can reappear in F2.

The test cross

A tall plant could be TT or Tt — both look tall. To find out, cross it with a dwarf (tt) plant. This is a test cross.

TT × tt → all Tall · · · Tt × tt → 1 Tall : 1 Dwarf

✍ Try it Yourself 1 (UDAAN)

A purple-flowered pea plant is crossed with a white-flowered plant. Half the F1 plants are purple and half are white. What is the genotype of the purple parent?

If purple parent were PP: PP × pp → all Pp (purple)
If purple parent is Pp: Pp × pp → 1 Pp (purple) : 1 pp (white) ✔

The purple parent is Pp. This is a test cross.

✍ NCERT Exercise 1

Tall plants with violet flowers are crossed with short plants with white flowers. All progeny have violet flowers, but almost half are short. The tall parent is: (a) TTWW (b) TTww (c) TtWW (d) TtWw

(c) TtWW. Half the progeny are short, so the tall parent must carry t (Tt). All progeny are violet, so the parent gives only W (WW).

✋ Test yourself 2

Different alleles are NOT: (a) determinants of characters (b) stretches of DNA (c) exact copies of each other (d) made of deoxyribonucleotides

Reveal the answer

(c) — alleles are different forms of a gene, so they are not exact copies.

Helix says: Here's my Punnett square trick. Write one parent's gametes on top, the other's down the side, and fill each box with one letter from each. For Tt crossed with Tt, three boxes have a capital T, so three out of four plants are tall!

✋ Activity 4.2

Mendel with two coins

Materials: two coins, a partner. Heads = T (tall allele), tails = t (dwarf allele). Each coin is one Tt parent making a gamete.

  1. Each partner tosses one coin; together the two coins make one offspring (e.g. heads + tails = Tt).
  2. Repeat 40 times and tally TT, Tt and tt.
  3. Count how many are tall (TT or Tt) and how many are dwarf (tt).
Reveal the observation and conclusion
Observation
The counts come out close to 10 TT : 20 Tt : 10 tt, about 30 tall : 10 dwarf — rarely exactly.
Conclusion
Each parent passes on one allele at random (segregation). Large numbers bring the result closer to 1 : 2 : 1 and 3 : 1 — that is why Mendel counted so many plants.
