Introduction

Litchi seeds grow into litchi plants. Cats give birth to kittens, never puppies. Yet no two kittens in a litter look exactly alike. Living things pass their features on to their young — but always with small differences. In this chapter we find out how features are passed on, and why the copies are never perfect.

Heredity and Genetics

◆ Key definitions

Heredity (inheritance) is the passing of characters from parents to their offspring, generation after generation.
Variation is the difference between individuals of the same species, and even between offspring of the same parents.
Genetics is the branch of biology that studies heredity and variation.

The information for every feature is stored in DNA. A segment of DNA that carries the information for one feature is called a gene.

💡 Knowledge Hub

The word genetics was coined by William Bateson in 1905. He was one of the scientists who spread Mendel’s work after it was rediscovered.

Accumulation of Variation During Reproduction

Every time a cell copies its DNA, the copy is almost perfect — but small errors creep in. Ultraviolet rays and some chemicals in pollutants can also change DNA. So every new generation carries the old differences plus a few new ones.

  • In asexual reproduction, the only variations come from small DNA-copying errors, so offspring are very similar to the single parent.
  • In sexual reproduction, DNA from two parents combines, so variation is much greater.
💡 Bacteria show it fast

Bacteria divide every few minutes, so millions of them appear in hours. If their food medium is changed, the few variants that can use the new food survive and soon take over the whole culture. In bigger organisms like fish or birds, the same kind of change takes far longer, because each generation lives much longer.

Why is variation useful?

🌡️Survive changesIf the environment changes (say, it gets hotter), some variants may cope better and survive.
🦠Resist diseaseIn a varied population, some individuals may resist a disease that kills others.
🐯Keep species aliveA small population (like the few remaining tigers) has little variation and can be wiped out by one disease.
🌱Healthy populationsInbreeding reduces variety and can lower fertility; a varied gene pool keeps populations healthy.
✍ NCERT — In-text question

If a trait A exists in 10% of a population of an asexually reproducing species and a trait B exists in 60% of the same population, which trait is likely to have arisen earlier?

Trait B. In asexual reproduction, a trait spreads only as its carriers keep dividing. A trait found in more individuals has had more generations to spread, so it most likely arose earlier.

Inherited and Acquired Traits

Features such as height, eye colour or the shape of the earlobe are called traits.

Inherited traitsAcquired traits
Controlled byGenes (DNA) of the germ cellsExperience, environment, training
Passed to offspring?YesNo
ExamplesFree or attached earlobes, eye colour, blood groupLosing a finger in an accident, a body-builder’s muscles, learning to dance
✍ NCERT — Why are acquired traits not inherited?

Acquired changes happen in somatic cells (body cells), not in the DNA of the germ cells (sperm and egg). Only germ-cell DNA reaches the next generation. A footballer’s skill, or a wrestler’s muscles, are not passed on to their children.

✋ Test yourself 1

Which of these is an inherited trait? (a) a scar from a fall (b) the ability to swim (c) attached earlobes (d) a tanned skin after a holiday

Reveal the answer

(c) attached earlobes — it is controlled by genes. The others are acquired during a lifetime.

Helix says: Hi, I'm Helix, a strand of DNA! Every time I'm copied, a tiny change can sneak in. Most changes are harmless, but over many generations they add up. That's why you look like your family, but you're still one of a kind!

✋ Activity 4.1

Earlobe survey

Materials: a notebook, and your classmates’ permission.

  1. Look at the lowest part of the ear. If it hangs loose, the earlobe is free; if it joins the side of the head, it is attached.
  2. Survey 20 classmates and count each type. Work out the percentage of each.
  3. Ask: is this trait inherited or acquired? Can it change during life?
Reveal the observation and conclusion
Observation
Both types are found in the class, usually in different numbers. Nobody can change their earlobe type by practice or exercise.
Conclusion
Earlobe type is an inherited trait — a natural variation within one species, controlled by genes received from parents.
